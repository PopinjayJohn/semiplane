## What every Warden here takes

A **feat**. Prerequisites, requirements and anything a pack can gate on are **data**,
so a feat is a row in the pack and a page in the vault, and neither one is derived
from the other.

Every Warden at this table takes it at second level, which is why the party has been
able to hold the north gate for three nights and is why nobody has slept. It is not a
good feat and it is not meant to be; it is the one that makes a Warden a Warden.

## What it does

You may hold one concentration effect indefinitely, at the cost of being unable to
speak. Not "one extra concentration" — the trade is deliberate and the trade is the
feat: a Warden who is holding the wall cannot report what is on the other side of it.

Which is exactly the shape of the thing this campaign is about. The Wardens have been
holding a gate for three generations and nobody wrote down what was beyond it, and the
feat is the reason that is expensive rather than impossible.

## Why it is a feat and not a spell

A feat is a permanent change to a character, so the pack keys it to a level and a
class. Semiplane's part is nothing at all: it renders a page whose `kind:` is `feat`,
and the pack's part is deciding what a feat *is*. [[lantern-light]] is the same shape
of thing with a different lifetime, and the difference is entirely the pack's.

## The house variant

Greyhaven plays one variant, and it is in the Game Master's notes rather than the
pack, because the pack is shared:

- Holding the effect costs nothing but the speech. A Warden who has held it for a
  whole night has a headache and no worse.
- It does not stack with itself. Two Wardens cannot hold the wall between them.

Both are **data in a page**, not code, and neither requires a rebuild of anything.
