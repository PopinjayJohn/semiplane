## You are not signed in

If you are reading this, you have reached a campaign you have never heard of, without
an account, without a cookie, and without anybody's permission. No sign-in prompt. No
membership. Every word on every page of this campaign, including the one you are on,
is available to a stranger who has the URL and nothing else.

That is not a setting somebody left on by accident. It is the campaign's declared
visibility, `public`, and it is the only thing this campaign is here to demonstrate.
Semiplane has three campaigns in its demo vault. One of them shows what the product
does when everything works. **This one shows exactly where it stops working**, because
a boundary you have read about is a boundary you can reason about, and a product that
only demonstrates its happy path teaches you nothing about the day it matters.

Hollowfield is a fen-side town that keeps its noticeboard in the open. Four pages
below, all reachable from here.

- [[town-notice]] — what is actually posted on an open board, and the one thing on it
  the Game Master has not put up yet.
- [[reading-with-no-account]] — the access matrix, sentence by sentence: what an
  anonymous reader gets, what is refused, and **why the refusal is a 401 and not a
  404**. This is the page worth reading.
- [[assets-follow-the-campaign]] — why a file in this campaign is readable by the same
  stranger who can read this sentence, and the mistake an operator makes about it.
- [[searching-without-signing-in]] — the search box over a campaign that anybody may
  read, and the one string it will never return.
- [[the-slash-and-the-line]] — a link out of this campaign into one you may not see,
  and two links that go nowhere on purpose.

## The one line that matters

A public campaign's **wiki** is open. Its **game** is not.

There is a Table in semiplane — the live tabletop, with a map and every token standing
on it — and it is the one surface a public campaign does not open. Every read of this
campaign's pages and files works for you. Attempting the tabletop asks you to sign in,
and says so in a way that is honest about the difference: the campaign itself is not a
secret to you, so the answer is "sign in to continue" rather than a page that claims
nothing is here.

[[reading-with-no-account]] has the request-by-request version, and it is a short read
because the rule behind it is one line of logic.

## What this campaign is not

It is not a small copy of the showcase. Almost nothing here is a *feature* demo,
because a feature demo is the showcase's job and duplicating it teaches the reader
nothing they could not get elsewhere. What is here is the set of questions an operator
actually gets wrong:

- does "public" mean "published" or does it mean "no login wall"?
- can a stranger fetch the map?
- can a stranger find this page by searching?
- what happens when a link leaves a campaign the reader may not see?

Answered on the pages linked above, with the request that produces each answer.
